Six-Solenoid Head

This head embosses a whole braille cell in one strike. Six solenoids stand in a ring, and each one pulls a lever. The levers lift six steel pins up through the paper into a metal die. The pins sit 2.5 mm apart, in 2 columns of 3.

The animation runs slower than a real head. Each solenoid draws about 2 A at 12 V while it is on. Firing a whole cell at once can draw up to 12 A. Firing one dot at a time draws at most 2 A, but takes one strike per dot.

What this head needs

  • Six levers. A solenoid is 22 mm wide, but the dots are 2.5 mm apart. So each solenoid reaches its pin through a lever. The lever turns the solenoid's 10 mm stroke into a 3 mm push at the pin, with about 3 times the force.
  • A metal die with six pockets. Each pin needs its own pocket, lined up to about 0.1 mm. M3 set screws are 3 mm wide, too wide to sit 2.5 mm apart. So the die is a drilled steel block.
  • Power. Up to 12 A when all six pins fire at once. One pin needs 2 A.
  • Space. The head is about 145 mm across, and all of it moves with the carriage.
  • Parts. The solenoids are cheap, about $3.30 each. Most of the cost is the levers, pins, die, a bigger power supply, and six copies of the switch circuit.

How fast it prints

These are estimates for a full page of text: 25 lines of 31 cells, with about 3 dots per cell. The test rig will give real numbers.
